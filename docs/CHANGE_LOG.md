# Changelog

All notable changes to the **Aliment** mod will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **A fever and the ache that comes with it are now on the HUD.** Everything the temperature did to a player used to be invisible: the weakness, the mining fatigue and the shimmer at the edges of the screen all arrived on their own, and the only way to find out how ill you were was to ask the diagnostic command. Two indicators say it instead, in the same row as the vanilla effects. **`aliment:fever`** has three grades, appearing at **38.5**, **39.5** and **40.0** °C, and **`aliment:pain`** is a single level from **39.5** °C.
  - Neither of them does anything. They carry no attribute modifier, they do not tick, and nothing in the mod reads them back - the weakness and the mining fatigue are exactly what they were. The self test asserts that, and it also asserts that the fever grade reads *higher* than the symptom tier it is judged against, so the two scales cannot quietly collapse into one.
  - The grades are a second scale rather than a re-cut of the symptom one. `thermalTier` still has exactly two fever steps, and the new middle grade at 39.5 changes nothing about what is done to the player - it is there because 39.5 is worth hearing about even though it is no worse than 38.6 was. The test pins the grade on both sides of all three thresholds, and separately requires the symptom tier to be identical either side of 39.5; if that ever stops holding, the grade has become a symptom and the middle step is what has to come back out.
  - They sit on the bar for exactly as long as the condition holds, with **no countdown**. A duration would put a timer next to the icon, and a timer answers the wrong question - what ends a fever is the body cooling down, not six seconds elapsing - so they are added with an infinite duration and taken off by hand the moment the temperature drops back under its threshold. That also closes a trap: vanilla keeps the *stronger* of two amplifiers when an effect is reapplied, so an implementation that only ever added would leave the icon reading **Fever III** after the fever had fallen back to **Fever I**.
  - Both are ordinary effect sprites found by the effect's own registry id, so they are drawn by a new generator, `tools/gen_effect_textures.ps1`, and named in all four languages.

### Changed
- **The CYP3A4 index now slides down instead of snapping.** Naringin was a *step function* on the enzyme, so the index only ever held one of five values and the whole inhibition arrived the instant a threshold was crossed. It is now a **curve**: the activity is exactly the calibrated value at the naringin it was calibrated for - **85** in a body that has eaten nothing, **60** at two slices, **45** at four, **25** at seven and **10** at eight and a half - and it interpolates between them, so one more slice always takes a little more off the liver rather than everything changing at once. Every calibrated value and every one of its naringin knots is unchanged; what is gone is the flat plateau between them. Each segment is a smoothstep, `3t^2 - 2t^3`, whose value and slope are both zero at either end, so the curve is flat *at* each knot and has no kink where two segments meet either. Past eight and a half slices it holds, which is still what makes that the deepest the inhibition goes.
  - The self test asserts the property rather than the table, because a table of five values cannot tell a curve from a staircase: it sweeps the whole naringin range in steps of a hundredth of a slice and requires the largest move the index makes to stay inside the steepest segment's own slope. The step function fails that outright, moving **25 points** in a single step. Alongside it, the curve is pinned to its six calibration points - including the flat run from eight and a half to the cap - and swept for any rise and any escape from the reported range.
- **Grapefruit consequently holds berberine down for longer.** The plateaus the index used to sit on are gone, so the liver runs lower through the middle of a dose: a single coptis herb still clears in about **9,400 ticks** on its own, but after nine slices of grapefruit it now takes about **20,100** rather than the 17,800 the step function gave - a little over twice as long instead of a little under. The shape of the interaction is unchanged, and so is every number it was calibrated from; only the descent between them is now a slope.
- **Peaceful difficulty now freezes the whole system, and the sugar-only hold is gone.** Peaceful used to hold *only* the blood glucose, on the reasoning that the sugar curve was the one thing in this mod that took hitpoints away by itself. That reasoning was too narrow: a potassium excess, the sepsis of a severe infection and its tissue damage all take hitpoints off with no difficulty setting to intercept them either. So the partial mechanism is deleted and peaceful is switched off through the same door creative mode already used - `AlimentSymptoms.isFrozen` is now `player.isCreative || player.level().difficulty == Difficulty.PEACEFUL`, one switch instead of two that could disagree about whether a body was being simulated at all.
  - Everything the hold did, it still does, because the model is simply not advanced: nothing moves on a peaceful body, food and injections included. What is new is everything *else* that used to keep running. No effect is applied, no damage is dealt, no mining penalty is charged and no exhaustion multiplier is applied.
  - What the mod has **already** put on the player comes back off, and three of those would otherwise never have come off at all. The two HUD indicators are added with an infinite duration and removed by hand, so a body frozen at a fever would have worn the icon indefinitely; the mining penalty is an **attribute modifier** with no lifetime of its own, so a player frozen mid-penalty would have kept the slower pickaxe forever. Both are cleared on the frozen path now. The ordinary vanilla effects are deliberately left to lapse instead of being cleared, because clearing them by hand would mean guessing which of them the mod was responsible for and would take the player's own potion of weakness with it.
  - The plumbing is gone rather than left unused. `tick` and `addGlucose` no longer take a hold flag on either side of the seam - the Scala model, `AlimentModelBridge`, and the Kotlin facade - so there is no way left to tick a body partially, and nothing is computed and then discarded. Every call site came out with it: the command's `statusLines` parameter and the `[held: peaceful]` tag it printed, the two ingestion and symptom checks, and the self tests built around them.
  - `/aliment status` therefore prints nothing about being frozen, which is a decision rather than an omission: the readout describes the body as stored, and a frozen body is one nothing is happening to rather than one whose numbers mean something different. A tag for it would have to go on every line to be honest, which is a readout explaining its own absence instead of the body.
  - The one consequence worth stating plainly is unchanged from the hold: a player who enters peaceful *already* crashing stays crashing, because the cause and the cure go off together. Nothing in the mod asks for damage on peaceful any more - it cannot, because the model is not advanced - so what keeps that from being lethal is `ServerPlayer.tickRegeneration`, which heals **1.0 HP a second** whenever health is below maximum. Disable `naturalRegeneration` and that arithmetic reverses, which is vanilla's business rather than this mod's.
  - The self test now checks the freeze from both sides, because every assertion in it is a negative one - nothing moved, nothing was applied, nothing left on the screen - and a body that was healthy to begin with would satisfy all of them while proving nothing. So the fixture's liveness is pinned separately (one tick really does move each axis, and a meal really does raise the sugar), and the freeze is released again at the end to prove the model resumes.

- **An electrolyte is now cleared faster the more of it there is.** The leak was one flat number per mineral, so a body carrying far too much sodium lost it at exactly the rate a healthy body lost its own - the excess could only be washed out by drinking, and nothing about the concentration itself mattered. The loss is now scaled by the value: the filtered load a kidney handles is what is in the blood, so `loss * excretion * (value / normal)` replaces `loss * excretion`. At normal the factor is exactly **1**, so the steady state and every existing calibration are untouched; what changes is that a high electrolyte leaves faster than a low one, and the higher it is the faster it leaves.
  - The per-mineral multipliers are unchanged, so the order they leave in is preserved - chloride and sodium first, magnesium and calcium last - and only the *rate* became concentration-dependent. The self test asserts the proportionality itself rather than a table of values: it puts two identical bodies at different concentrations, ticks both, and requires the higher one to have lost more in absolute terms.
- **Iodine deficiency now needs to be deeper before it shows.** The reference floor is **0.25**, down from 0.40, so a player gets *no* symptom at all until iodine has fallen halfway from normal to the hard floor. The thyroid is a late responder, and a deficiency that produced symptoms at 0.39 was saying a great deal about a body that had barely lost anything. Everything the thyroid drives reads the one number, so the set point, the deficit symptom and the status readout all move together, and severe hypothyroidism still waits for its own threshold at 0.20.
- **Iodine is weaker across the board, and takes longer to run out.** Every dietary source is **halved** - kelp 0.05, dried kelp and raw seaweed 0.10, cooked seaweed 0.125, seaweed iodized salt 0.20, a Farmer's Delight kelp roll 0.15 and a slice 0.05 - and the store now drains from 0.50 to the 0.05 floor in **5 game days** rather than 3, a leak of **0.09** a day. Two kelp a day is still the maintenance diet and one is still not enough, so the shape of the choice is unchanged; what is gone is the run of three days in which a single missed helping was harmless.
  - A surplus is now cleared faster than the leak, in proportion to how far above normal it is, so an excess drains away sooner than the flat rate would manage **without bending the depletion curve**: the extra term is written against the excess above normal rather than against the whole value, which is what keeps "five days from 0.50 to the floor" an exact figure. The self test pins both halves - the flat five days from normal, and a surplus losing more than a normal store over the same ticks - because one number cannot hold both claims.

### Fixed
- **`/aliment status` now really does print every metric.** It is documented as *every physiological metric, in one readout*, and it had drifted a long way from that: the whole **glucose chain** - the glucose itself, the body's own insulin and injected insulin aspart - was missing from it, and so was the **liver**, meaning both the naringin load and the CYP3A4 index the grapefruit interaction turns on. All four are quantities the model runs on and the player had no way to see. They are on the readout now, the glucose with its reference-range tag and the liver pair printed directly under the berberine it clears, so that "the coptis is lasting because of the grapefruit" reads in one place instead of two.
  - Three more things were missing for the same reason: the two new **indicator** effects with their grades, and the **immune-response latch** - the flag that says the body has noticed an infection. That one trips well before the load is high enough to make the player feel ill, and it is the reason inflammation can be climbing during a stretch where nothing appears to be wrong.
  - The **condition line** had the same hole from the other side: a body in a hypoglycaemic crash was announced as `healthy`, because the ladder never asked about glucose. It does now, and the crash gets its own rung ahead of the electrolyte imbalances - it is the only state on that ladder that takes hitpoints off by itself.
  - The line building is split out of the command handler as `statusLines(data)`, for the same reason the glucose meter's message is: so the self test can assert the text the command really produces rather than one it built for itself. The assertion that earns its place is **completeness** - the test reads the fields `AlimentData` actually declares, pairs each one with a fragment of text only its own line can produce, and requires every one of those fragments to be present in the output. A field added to the state without a line here now fails the test by name instead of quietly going unprinted, which is precisely how the glucose chain went missing in the first place. (The first version of that check compared the field list against a second list of field names, which no edit to the readout could ever have failed - deleting the liver line left it green. It is the fragment, matched against the returned text, that makes it a claim about the readout.)
  - Note that `/aliment set` still cannot touch glucose, insulin or naringin; the readout is complete before the setter is.
